App registration

  • It must be the SPA platform, not Web — a Web registration rejects the token exchange with a cross-origin redemption error.
  • MSAL cannot use file://; serve the page over http(s).
2
Required permissions

Microsoft Graph → Delegated, read-only, admin consent

Application.Read.All app registrations and service principals
DelegatedPermissionGrant.Read.All delegated consent grants

Nothing here writes. These two are the floor — without them the scan cannot start.

3
Optional permissions — each degrades gracefully

Absent ones are reported as not assessed rather than as a clean result.

RoleManagement.Read.Directory Entra directory roles held by service principals — the authorisation behind a capability permission like Exchange.ManageAsApp, which does nothing without it
AuditLog.Read.All usage detection — whether and when each app actually authenticated app-only, and with which credential (sign-in logs need Entra ID P1/P2)
Application.ReadWrite.All only to edit an app's redirect URIs from the detail drawer

The write scope is the one exception to read-only, and it is asked for at the moment you use it rather than at sign-in — so a session that edits nothing never requests it.

4
Directory role

Delegated tokens act as the signed-in user, so the permissions above grant nothing on their own — the account needs its own read access.

  • Global Reader is enough for everything on this page, and grants no write anywhere in the tenant.
  • Runs entirely in your browser, read-only; nothing is sent anywhere except Microsoft Graph.

Highest-risk combination hide ▴

One compound outranks everything else: a multi-tenant app registered in another tenant holding application permissions — app-only access with no signed-in user, no Conditional Access and no session to expire, on a credential that lives in the publisher’s tenant and that you cannot rotate. A high-privilege scope and an unverified publisher are the escalators on top of that. Disabled service principals are excluded — they cannot authenticate, so their key is dormant and no remediation would ever clear them from the count; they stay in the table at their own rating.
Read the row left to right as a single AND. Colour is the severity the chain has reached — amber once an app is externally registered, red once it also holds application permissions, and a green zero where the AND leaves nobody behind. Application permissions and the credentials behind them hide ▴

Every distinct application permission granted in this view, worst first, and what its holders authenticate with. An application permission is only as protected as the key in front of it, and a client secret is the weakest of them — a copyable string that lives wherever the publisher put it. Credentials can only be read for apps registered in your tenant; for externally-registered apps the key sits in the publisher’s tenant, so those holders are counted in their own column and cannot be inspected or rotated by you. Click a row to scope the table to the apps holding that permission.
